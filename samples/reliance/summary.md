## Management commentary

- Reliance group reported record consolidated quarterly EBITDA of Rs. 62,409 Crores (up 24% YoY) and PAT of close to Rs. 30,000 Crores (up 34% YoY), led primarily by strong O2C performance.
  > It was an EBITDA of Rs.62,409 Crores, which is 24% up on a year - on - year basis. This is the highest EBITDA that we have declared for consol and simultaneously, the PAT also at close to Rs.30,000 Crores, up 34%.
- The Jio Platforms IPO achieved a first day listing valuation of almost $137 billion, making Jio the second most valuable listed company in India.
  > The IPO was absolutely a defining moment, making Jio the second most valuable company. It is particularly nice that it follows Reliance, which is the largest market cap company, and first day listing of almost $137 billion in terms of value.
- Reliance Retail is deliberately trading off short-term earnings growth to build a scalable digital commerce platform funded by store cash flows.
  > We have been trading off short term earnings growth to build a very scalable digital commerce platform. And the cash flow from our store business, in a way, is funding digital commerce growth.
- Ethane-naphtha cracking differential widened significantly to $557 in Q2 (up 2.3x YoY), creating a strong advantage for Reliance's ethane feedstock cracking operations.
  > Ethane prizes were steady as I said, and ethane and naphtha cracking differential therefore widened to $557 in Q2, and this was up 2.3 times from a year - on - year basis.

**Tone: positive** - Management highlighted all-time high consolidated quarterly EBITDA of Rs. 62,409 Crores (up 24% YoY) and PAT growth of 34% YoY, backed by strong performances across major business segments.

## Segment performance

- **O2C (Oil to Chemicals):** EBITDA rose 69% YoY to Rs. 25,203 Crores, driven by elevated downstream fuel cracks, operational agility, and favorable ethane feedstock differentials.
  > Rs.25,203 Crores in terms of O2C EBITDA. As I mentioned, 69% higher, one of the strongest that we have seen, benefiting from the tight fuel markets and the advantage of fuel sourcing.
- **Jio Platforms / Digital Services:** Operating revenues grew 11.4% YoY to Rs. 40,475 Crores, while EBITDA grew 16.6% YoY to Rs. 21,879 Crores with strong subscriber growth and expanding ARPU.
  > Operating revenues of Rs.40,475 Crores, 11.4% year - on - year increase. EBITDA showed 16.6% year - on - year increase at Rs.21,879 Crores
- **Reliance Retail:** Revenue grew 6.4% YoY; EBITDA before exceptionals was Rs. 6,228 Crores. Recorded a Rs. 108 Crore exceptional charge for shutting down 7-Eleven.
  > revenue grew at a rate of 6.4% on a Y - o - Y basis. ... EBITDA of Rs. 6,228 Crores. This is before a one - time exceptional charge on account of 7 - eleven of Rs.108 Crores because as you might have seen in the news, we have shut down the 7 - eleven format.
- **FMCG (Reliance Consumer Products):** Delivered Q2 revenue of Rs. 8,266 Crores (1.6x YoY growth) and H1 revenue of Rs. 16,900 Crores, driven by daily essentials and Campa beverages.
  > So, in Q2, we delivered a revenue of Rs.8,266 Crores, which is 1.6 times year - on - year growth right now. For the first half, our revenue was Rs.16,900 Crores, again, 1.8 times of the last year's first half.
- **JioStar (Media):** Exited Q2 with 440 million MAUs on JioHotstar (up 10% YoY) and achieved 10% YoY revenue growth led by subscription and ad revenue expansion.
  > In this quarter, we exited the quarter with a total MAU of 440 million, comparable to 400 million in the same quarter last year. So, that is a 10% year - on - year growth. ... we ended the quarter with a 10% year - on - year revenue growth that was driven primarily by very strong subscription growth
- **Exploration & Production:** Production sustained at ~25 MMSCMD; natural field decline in KG-D6 offset by elevated oil/gas prices, exchange rates, and CBM uptrend.
  > So, we stand at about 25 million standard cubic meters. We will continue to see an uptrend in CBM production. We are trying to manage the decline in D6 and sustain the production as far as possible.

## Notable Q&A

- **Q (Probal Sen – ICICI Securities):** Is it fair to assume that the 5 million barrels of affected global refinery capacity is six to nine months away from normalization?
  **A:** Sh Srinivas Tuttagunta agreed with the assumption, stating high geopolitical uncertainty, continued attacks in Hormuz/Red Sea, and low inventories suggest no immediate resolution.
  > is it fair to assume this 5 million barrels is probably six to nine months away from normalization in a best case scenario, and worst case it could be it could stretch for even longer? ... Yes. I think, what you said, I agree with that.
- **Q (Jal Irani – Nuvama):** Where did the significant sequential O2C profit growth come from—refineries, petrochemicals, or additional ethane carriers?
  **A:** Sh Srinivas Tuttagunta explained it was a combined effort; petchem contributed significantly, refining diesel cracks improved ~$2.8/bbl sequentially, gasifier utilization provided margin uplift, and TC fleet mitigated freight risks.
  > I am just wondering among the three which was the main pivotal can you give us a more in - depth sense? ... I kind of, will say two or three things. I think it is a combined effect, but if you have to put a number , petrochemicals definitely have contributed, but refining also if you look at diesel, I think it is almost $2.8 a barrel higher sequentially.
- **Q (Sanjay Mookim – J.P. Morgan):** Can we get an update on the commissioning timeline for the new energy lines?
  **A:** Sh Karan Suri shared that module capacity (6+ GW now) and solar cell (5 GW now) will both reach 20 GW in early/H1 next year. BESS pack/container facility (up to 40 GWh) will commission next calendar half year, and cell manufacturing (35-40 GWh) will come late next year. ⚠ check numbers
  > Can we get a quick update on the commissioning of the various new energy lines, please? ... When it comes to the solar value chain, as indicated, I think our module capacity is now six plus gigawatt, and we will be achieving up to 20 gigawatt early next year. Similarly, solar cell is now nearing around 5 - gigawatt and we will be achieving again 20 - gigawatt capacity sometime first half next year.
- **Q (Sachin Salgaonkar – Bank of America):** Is Reliance keen on bidding for IPL media rights when up for renewal in early 2027?
  **A:** Sh V Srikanth and Sh Ishan Chatterjee affirmed interest, stating marquee sports properties remain core to strategic agenda, but bidding will strictly adhere to group principles of financial discipline and profitable growth. ⚠ check numbers
  > IPL bidding is this something you guys are keen? ... The answer is absolutely yes. At what price? That is a different one. ... IPL and marquee properties like this are a very core part of our strategic agenda, but we will address this objectively once the tender comes out, keeping the group’s philosophy in mind of profitable growth.

## Risks / concerns

- O2C business experienced elevated OpEx due to high crude premiums, surge in freight rates, and increased insurance costs.
  > And what is particularly impressive is that it came on the back of significantly elevated OpEx, the kind of crude premiums that we saw, the kind of surge in freight rates, insurance costs.
- Retail discretionary demand faces pressure from a 2-year high inflation rate, rupee depreciation raising imported product costs, and monsoon deficits in key agricultural states.
  > Inflation is on a two year high, which is impacting the purchasing power for people. Currency has depreciated significantly. That is impacting, especially, some of the imported products. The landed product s have gone up. Monsoon deficit. So that has caused some demand pressure across some of the key agri dependent states
- Geopolitical conflict in the Middle East and Russia-Ukraine severely disrupted global refinery and chemical manufacturing capacity, impacting raw material availability.
  > Lot of capacity global capacity was impacted because of the war, two wars actually: Middle East war and the R ussia Ukraine. Polyethylene almost 41 million tonnes, which is roughly about 30% of the global capacity.
